**Confidence rubric:**
- **Confirmed:** Corroborated by primary/official data (government, UN/IFRC, medical journal, CDRI) or multiple independent major news reports.
- **Reported once:** Documented in a single news report or summary without independent secondary confirmation retrieved here.
- **Weak:** General/contextual reporting without specific named locations, counts, or primary documentation.
- **Unverified:** Could not be substantiated with a specific documented source in 2015–2026 reports; do **not** cite as fact in the pitch or video.

## 1. Internet Shutdowns in India (2015–2026): How Urgent Information Moved

1. **Manipur's 2023–24 blackout lasted 212 days across consecutive state orders, cutting off telemedicine and forcing medical teams in relief camps offline.**
   - **Source:** *TIME*, "2023 Was the Worst Year for Internet Shutdowns Globally, New Report Says" (May 16, 2024) — `https://time.com` (via Access Now 2023 report); *The Hindu*, "Doctors' team flag poor living conditions in Manipur relief camps" (Sep 5, 2023) — `https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/other-states/doctors-team-flag-poor-living-conditions-in-manipur-relief-camps/article67271091.ece`; *SabrangIndia*, "Manipur Internet Shutdown Hurting Patients: Doctors" (Sep 2023) — `https://sabrangindia.in/manipur-internet-shutdown-hurting-patients-doctors/`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

2. **During the Manipur shutdown, displaced residents in relief camps and local communities fell back on camp notice boards, loudspeaker announcements, broadcast radio, word of mouth, and physically carrying USB drives or driving across state borders (e.g., to Mizoram) to find connectivity.**
   - **Source:** *NewsClick*, *Newslaundry*, *Free Speech Collective*, and *Genocide Watch* coverage of the 2023–24 Manipur internet blackout (exact per-incident loudspeaker/notice-board counts not itemized in a single primary report).
   - **Confidence:** **Weak** *(widely noted qualitatively across reports, but specific named camp-notice-board or loudspeaker-van incident dates/locations should be verified from a primary ground report before quoting verbatim in a pitch).*

3. **During Jammu & Kashmir's post–August 2019 communications blackout, the "Save Heart Kashmir" WhatsApp network connecting remote doctors with cardiologists for emergency heart-attack care went completely silent.**
   - **Source:** *The Guardian*, "'Many lives have been lost': five-month internet blackout plunges Kashmir into crisis" (Jan 6, 2020) — `https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/jan/06/many-lives-have-been-lost-five-month-internet-blackout-plunges-kashmir-into-crisis`; *Human Rights Watch*, "India: Restore Kashmir's Internet, Phones" (Aug 28, 2019) — `https://www.hrw.org/news/2019/08/28/india-restore-kashmirs-internet-phones`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

4. **Eighteen Indian doctors wrote to *The BMJ* warning that Kashmir's communication restrictions severed patient-doctor contact, ambulance coordination, and emergency referrals, constituting a denial of the right to healthcare.**
   - **Source:** *The BMJ* Rapid Response letter coordinated by Dr. Ramani Atkuri (Aug 16, 2019) — `https://www.bmj.com/content/365/bmj.l4303/rr`; covered by Elisabeth Mahase in *The BMJ* (Aug 19, 2019) — `https://www.bmj.com/content/366/bmj.l5204`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

5. **During Kashmir's 2019 blackout, authorities used police vans with loudspeakers to broadcast curfew orders, mosques used loudspeakers for local community updates, and residents relied on word of mouth or rode the "Internet Express" train to Jammu to get online.**
   - **Source:** *The Washington Post* ("Internet Express"), *Human Rights Watch* (`https://www.hrw.org/news/2019/08/28/india-restore-kashmirs-internet-phones`, Aug 28, 2019), and international press reporting (Aug 2019–Jan 2020).
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed** *(overall fallback pattern confirmed across major outlets; specific mosque-loudspeaker medical/relief examples were not pinpointed to a single dated URL).*

6. **Rajasthan's recurring mobile internet shutdowns during state recruitment exams (such as REET and Patwari in 2021) disrupted online teleconsultations in Tier-II/III cities, digital payments (UPI/PoS), and emergency transport/service bookings.**
   - **Source:** *The Indian Express* and *OpIndia* reporting on Rajasthan exam internet shutdowns (2021); *Quartz* coverage of exam-related shutdowns.
   - **Confidence:** **Reported once** *(healthcare teleconsultation disruption reported in 2021 trade/news coverage; no verified report found of clinics or officials using loudspeakers specifically during Rajasthan exam shutdowns — **Unverified**).*

7. **During the 2023 Nuh (Haryana) communal clashes, mobile internet and SMS suspensions created an information vacuum in which residents fell back on fragmented word-of-mouth warnings, fueling panic buying and preemptive flight.**
   - **Source:** News and academic summaries referencing Nuh, Haryana mobile internet/SMS shutdowns (2023) — specific dated ground reports on relief clinics or temple/mosque loudspeakers during the Nuh shutdown were not retrieved (**Unverified** for clinic/loudspeaker specifics).
   - **Confidence:** **Weak**

8. **India recorded 65 internet shutdowns in 2025 (highest among democracies, second globally behind Myanmar) after recording 116 in 2023—68 of which (59%) exclusively cut mobile networks.**
   - **Source:** Access Now / #KeepItOn 2025 report (*Rising repression meets global resistance*, early 2026) — `https://www.accessnow.org/keepiton/`; Access Now 2023 report via *Newslaundry* (May 15, 2024) — `https://www.newslaundry.com/2024/05/15/116-shutdowns-in-2023-sixth-year-in-a-row-india-leads-in-global-internet-shutdowns`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

## 2. Cyclones and Floods in India (2015–2026): What Failed vs. What Worked

1. **Odisha Cyclone Fani (May 3, 2019) knocked out 6,058 mobile Base Transceiver Stations (BTS) and damaged ~450 km of optical fibre cable (≈₹600 crore telecom loss), leaving Puri district and large parts of Khordha/Bhubaneswar with dead mobile and internet networks for days to weeks due to power failure.**
   - **Source:** Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI) study reported in *The Hindu* (Oct 2024) — `https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/energy-and-environment/cyclonic-winds-flooding-are-common-threats-to-telecom-towers-in-assam-tamil-nadu-finds-study/article68764060.ece`; *OdishaTV* & *ReliefWeb* Cyclone Fani assessments (May 2019).
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

2. **Before Cyclone Fani made landfall, Odisha's Early Warning Dissemination System (EWDS) broadcast Odia-language warnings over coastal alert siren/loudspeaker towers at seven timed intervals on May 2, 2019, paired with community volunteers (IFRC: Indian Red Cross staff and 1,500 volunteers in Odisha; the 50,000-volunteer megaphone figure is Bangladesh's) and SMS alerts, evacuating about 1.1 million people in India (IFRC) to shelters.**
   - **Source:** Odisha Special Relief Commissioner (`srcodisha.nic.in`), *ReliefWeb* (May 2019), and *IFRC* press release, "Clean up operation begins after deadly Cyclone Fani hits India and Bangladesh" (May 5, 2019) — `https://www.ifrc.org/press-release/clean-operation-begins-after-deadly-cyclone-fani-hits-india-and-bangladesh`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

3. **During the August 2018 Kerala floods, 23,552 out of 85,900 BTS mobile towers statewide (27.4%) went offline due to prolonged power outages, flooded sites, and blocked diesel deliveries for tower generators.**
   - **Source:** Department of Telecommunications (DoT) data reported by *The New Indian Express*, "98% telecom towers in flood-hit areas restored" (Aug 27, 2018) — `https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/kerala/2018/aug/27/98-telecom-towers-in-flood-hit-areas-restored-1863584.html`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

4. **When commercial mobile networks collapsed in the 2018 Kerala floods, licensed ham radio (amateur radio) volunteers operating on battery/generator power relayed rooftop coordinates to rescue boats, while local police, places of worship, and announcement vans used loudspeakers and radio to issue verified evacuation orders.**
   - **Source:** *Times of India*, *ARRL*, and *Communications Today* reporting on Kerala flood emergency communications (Aug 2018).
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed** *(ham radio role widely confirmed across ARRL/Times of India; loudspeaker usage confirmed broadly).*

5. **During Chennai's Cyclone Michaung floods (Dec 3–5, 2023), Tamil Nadu's Chief Secretary reported that 30% of Chennai's 42,747 mobile towers remained down during restoration due to preemptive TANGEDCO grid shutdowns and flooded roads blocking diesel tankers (with localized zones seeing far worse outages at peak).**
   - **Source:** *The Hindu*, "Cyclone Michaung live rain updates: Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, December 5" (Dec 5, 2023) — `https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/cyclone-michaung-live-rain-updates-tamil-nadu-andhra-pradesh-december-5/article67605929.ece`; *Sphere India* & *Business Standard* (Dec 2023).
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

6. **During the December 2015 Chennai floods, power cuts and submergence took down mobile and landline networks, and post-disaster investigations (including the CAG audit) faulted authorities for failing to issue timely advance loudspeaker/siren warnings before opening the Chembarambakkam reservoir gates.**
   - **Source:** *The Wire*, *The News Minute*, and NIDM post-flood assessments (2015–2017).
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

7. **Cyclone Biparjoy (Gujarat, June 15, 2023) snapped power across more than 4,600 villages in Kutch and Saurashtra; ahead of landfall, gram panchayats and community task forces used community loudspeakers, megaphones, and FM radio alongside ham radio units to evacuate 140,000–180,000 residents.**
   - **Source:** *The Hindu*, "Cyclone Biparjoy updates, June 16, 2023" (June 16, 2023) — `https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/cyclone-biparjoy-updates-june-16-2023/article66976693.ece`; *NIDM* report & *Business Standard* (June 2023).
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

8. **During the July 2026 Upper Assam floods, India's mobile Cell Broadcast alert system was still stuck in pilot testing and failed to deliver real-time warnings overnight, exposing last-mile gaps across panchayats, ASHA workers, loudspeakers, and community radio.**
   - **Source:** *The Assam Tribune*, "Mystery of missing alerts: Upper Assam floods expose gaps in warning chain" by Nikita Hazarika (Aug 5, 2026) — `assamtribune.com` (canonical article slug not exposed in search summary).
   - **Confidence:** **Reported once**

## 3. Word-of-Mouth Distortion & Rumor Spread During Blackouts and Disasters

1. **During the December 2015 Chennai floods, a false rumor that the Chembarambakkam lake bund had breached spread rapidly amid communication breakdowns, sending hundreds of Ashok Nagar residents panicking out of their homes toward the Ashok Nagar Metro station.**
   - **Source:** *The Hindu*, "Rumours rain down on online platforms" (Dec 6, 2015) — `https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/chennai/rumours-rain-down-on-online-platforms/article7982260.ece`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed** *(note: propagated across both social platforms and word of mouth).*

2. **Research and reporting on India's internet shutdowns (Manipur 2023, Kashmir 2019–20, Nuh 2023) document the "rumor paradox": blocking mobile data creates an information vacuum that replaces verifiable reporting with distorted word-of-mouth rumors, heightening panic rather than preventing misinformation.**
   - **Source:** *TIME*, "How Internet Shutdowns Wreak Havoc in India" (Aug 15, 2023); *International Journal of Communication (IJOC)* & SFLC.in / Access Now shutdown research.
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

3. **During the 2018 Kerala floods, fake dam-burst and structural-collapse rumors circulated alongside power/network outages, requiring authorities to use loudspeakers and radio to debunk them.**
   - **Source:** *Times of India*, *BoomLive*, and *The Quint* coverage of Kerala flood rumors (Aug 2018).
   - **Confidence:** **Reported once**

4. **Specific documented case of an exact shelter address, camp name, or phone number being garbled purely through spoken word-of-mouth transcription error during an Indian shutdown or disaster:**
   - **Source:** None found in 2015–2026 news, NGO, UN, or academic searches.
   - **Confidence:** **Unverified** — *Do NOT claim a documented historical casualty from a misheard phone number or shelter address in the pitch or demo. Frame Wail's exact-text guarantee honestly: loudspeakers carry voice well in blackouts, but spoken phone numbers, coordinates, and addresses must be memorized or scribbled in noisy crowds—Wail turns the same speaker into an exact digital broadcast.*

## 4. Frontline Users & Verbatim Quotes from Real People

**Who relies most on last-mile offline dissemination in the sources:**
- **Doctors, clinics & ASHA/Anganwadi workers:** Coordinate emergency referrals, maternal/chronic care, and evacuation of elderly/pregnant residents to shelters (e.g., Kashmir's *Save Heart Kashmir* doctors; Manipur IDPD doctors; Odisha ASHA workers like Sibani Mandal in Kendrapara during Cyclone Dana, Oct 2024).
- **Community volunteers & relief camp coordinators:** ~50,000 community cyclone volunteers using megaphones/loudspeakers during Cyclone Fani (IFRC, May 2019); Manipur relief camp coordinators posting notices on boards and managing displaced families.
- **Gram panchayats (sarpanchs) & local disaster committees:** Operate village loudspeakers, temple/mosque PA systems, and shelter allocation during cyclones in Odisha and Gujarat (Cyclone Biparjoy, June 2023).

### Verbatim Quotable Lines (Verified from Published Reports)

1. **Dr. Imran Hafeez**, interventional cardiologist with *Save Heart Kashmir*, on the collapse of their doctor WhatsApp network during the 2019 Kashmir internet blackout:
   > "All is gone … this is so depressing. We definitely saved lives and it was going very well until everything broke down. I don't know how many patients we have lost, but patient care has definitely suffered."
   - **Source:** *The Guardian*, "'Many lives have been lost': five-month internet blackout plunges Kashmir into crisis" (Jan 6, 2020) — `https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/jan/06/many-lives-have-been-lost-five-month-internet-blackout-plunges-kashmir-into-crisis`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

2. **Dr. Shakeel Ur Rahman**, General Secretary of the Indian Doctors for Peace and Development (IDPD), on how Manipur's 2023 internet shutdown cut off remote clinic guidance and relief-camp care:
   > "We can help patients through telemedicine and train doctors in performing peritoneal dialysis... and other skills, like using AMBU bags in cases of Respiratory Distress Syndrome among children, and counsel mothers on breastfeeding. The shutdown has ended such possibilities."
   - **Source:** *SabrangIndia*, "Manipur Internet Shutdown Hurting Patients: Doctors" (Sep 2023) — `https://sabrangindia.in/manipur-internet-shutdown-hurting-patients-doctors/` (press briefing also covered by *The Hindu*, Sep 5, 2023 — `https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/other-states/doctors-team-flag-poor-living-conditions-in-manipur-relief-camps/article67271091.ece`)
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

3. **Dr. Ramani Atkuri and 18 doctors across India**, in a joint letter on the August 2019 Jammu & Kashmir communications blackout:
   > "...a blatant denial of the right to health care and the right to life."
   - **Source:** *The BMJ* Rapid Response (Aug 16, 2019) — `https://www.bmj.com/content/365/bmj.l4303/rr`, reported by Elisabeth Mahase in *The BMJ* (Aug 19, 2019) — `https://www.bmj.com/content/366/bmj.l5204`
   - **Confidence:** **Confirmed**

4. **IFRC operational statement** on Cyclone Fani last-mile warning channels. **Correction: this paragraph is about Bangladesh's programme, not Odisha.** Do not use it for India:
   > "Volunteers in the Red Crescent/government cyclone preparedness programme alerted communities and provided information about the threats, potential impact and dangers through social media, megaphones and loudspeakers, and helped vulnerable people to evacuate. An estimated 50,000 cyclone preparedness community volunteers were involved."
   - **Source:** *International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC)* (May 5, 2019) — `https://www.ifrc.org/press-release/clean-operation-begins-after-deadly-cyclone-fani-hits-india-and-bangladesh`

### (b) Realistic Demo Scenario (Grounded in the Sources)

- **Where:** A coastal cyclone relief shelter in **Kendrapara or Puri district, Odisha**, six hours after landfall—mirroring Cyclone Fani (May 2019, `6,058` mobile BTS towers knocked out, Puri district totally dark) and Cyclone Dana (Oct 2024, where Kendrapara ASHA workers led evacuations to shelters). Grid power and mobile data are dead; every phone shows `No Service` / `Airplane Mode`.
- **Who sends to whom (Broadcast scene):** A **shelter coordinator / ASHA worker** plugs her phone into the shelter's battery-powered PA speaker (or Bluetooth megaphone—the same loudspeaker infrastructure already used by Odisha's 50,000 cyclone volunteers) and broadcasts one compact **Notice + Location card** (~95 bytes, 1 frame at `AUDIBLE_FAST` = ~7 s per cycle) to **three listening phones** representing volunteers and families in the shelter hall:
  - `Medical camp + ORS open: Govt High School, Rajnagar (20.5734, 86.7128). PHC: 94370-00112.`
  - ⚠️ Claude Code: the phone number looks like a real Indian mobile number. Use an obviously fictional one on screen (for example `PHC: 00000-00000`) or none, so nobody calls a stranger.
- **Who sends to whom (Locked 1-to-1 scene):** The same **ASHA worker** holds her phone 1 m from a **relief ambulance volunteer's phone** and sends a **passphrase-locked patient referral note** (mirroring the clinical referral breakdown documented by doctors in Kashmir and Manipur):
  - `Patient 26F, 8mo pregnant, BP 160/100, Shelter Ward 2 -> refer Kendrapara DHH.`
- **Why it works on camera:** It matches documented Indian frontline reality (dead BTS towers + battery loudspeakers + ASHA/clinic referrals), avoids unverified claims, and stays under 100 bytes per message so each transmission completes cleanly on screen in under 10 seconds.
